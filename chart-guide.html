How to read a chart.

Charts look intimidating until someone breaks them down. Here's the plain-English version: what the colours mean, what the sticks are telling you, and how to spot a trend.

01Anatomy of a candlestick

Each "candle" represents price movement over a set period (a day, an hour, a minute). It packs four pieces of information into one shape: the open, close, high, and low price for that period.

The body is the thick part. It shows the gap between the opening and closing price for the period.
The wick (or shadow) is the thin line above and below. It marks the highest and lowest price reached.
Green means the price closed higher than it opened. Buyers were in control.
Red means the price closed lower than it opened. Sellers were in control.
02Green vs red, side by side

The colour tells you the direction at a glance. The position of the open and close flips depending on whether the period ended up or down.

Quick tip: a long body means a big move that period. A short body means the open and close were close together, signalling indecision.
03What the wicks tell you

Wicks reveal the fight between buyers and sellers. A long wick shows price went somewhere then got pushed back, which can hint at where momentum is shifting.

Long lower wick: price dropped but buyers pushed it back up. Can signal buying interest at lower prices.
Long upper wick: price rose but sellers pushed it back down. Can signal selling pressure at higher prices.
Doji (tiny body): open and close are nearly equal. The market is undecided. Often appears before a change in direction.
Remember: a single candle is one data point. Patterns become meaningful only in the context of what came before and after. No candle is a guarantee.
04Reading the trend

Zoom out from individual candles and you see the bigger picture. The trend is the general direction price is moving over time. There are three: up, down, and sideways.

Uptrend: a series of higher highs and higher lows. The overall direction is up. Buyers are dominant.
Downtrend: a series of lower highs and lower lows. The overall direction is down. Sellers are dominant.
Sideways: price bounces between a ceiling and a floor without clear direction. The market is consolidating.
05Support and resistance

These are price levels where the market has historically reacted. Support is a floor where buying tends to appear. Resistance is a ceiling where selling tends to appear.

Why it matters: traders watch these levels closely. When price breaks through resistance or falls below support, it can signal a shift. But levels break all the time, so they are guides, not rules.
